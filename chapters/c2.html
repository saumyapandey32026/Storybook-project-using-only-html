Chapter 2

The Lion and the Rabbit (Panchatantra)


The Panchatantra story of the Lion and the Rabbit tells of a fierce, arrogant lion named Bhasuraka who killed animals mercilessly. The forest animals agreed to send one animal daily as food to stop his reckless hunting. When it was a clever rabbit's turn, he arrived late, claimed another lion threatened him, and led the proud lion to a deep well. Seeing his reflection, the lion jumped in to fight his "rival" and died.

Key Aspects of the Story:

The Problem:

The lion is greedy and kills more animals than he can eat, prompting the animals to make a deal to send one animal per day.

The Conflict:

A small rabbit, tasked with being the meal, decides to save himself and the other animals. He arrives late to the lion's den, making the lion hungry and furious.

The Trick:

The rabbit tells the lion that another stronger lion is challenging his authority in the forest.

The Climax:

The rabbit leads the angry lion to a deep, narrow well and shows him his reflection in the water. The Resolution: The lion, thinking the reflection is the rival, roars and jumps into the well to fight, falling to his death

Moral of the Story:

Wisdom is stronger than physical strength. Intelligence can conquer brute force. This story highlights that arrogance and pride (the lion) can easily be defeated by intelligence and cunning (the rabbit).
