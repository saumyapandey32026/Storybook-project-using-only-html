The monkey and the crocodile (panchatantra)


A monkey living on a jamun tree befriended a crocodile, sharing fruit daily. The crocodile's greedy wife demanded the monkey’s heart, so the crocodile attempted to drown him. The quick-witted monkey claimed he left his heart on the tree, tricking the crocodile into returning to shore, where the monkey escaped.

The Story of the Monkey and the Crocodile

The Friendship:

A monkey lived on a lush tree, often depicted as a jamun (Java plum) tree, on the bank of a river. He spent his days eating fruit. A crocodile lived in the river, and the two became friends, with the monkey sharing fruit with the crocodile.

The Deceit:

The crocodile took some fruits to his wife. She decided that if the monkey ate such sweet fruit every day, his heart must be even sweeter, and demanded to eat it.

The Trap:

Reluctantly, the crocodile invited the monkey to his house, pretending to offer him lunch, while intending to kill him. The monkey, who could not swim, agreed to sit on the crocodile's back.

The Truth and Escape:

Midway across the river, the crocodile confessed the plan. The clever monkey did not panic. He told the crocodile, "Oh, why didn't you tell me earlier? I keep my heart safe on the tree!".

The Climax:

The foolish crocodile turned back. Upon reaching the bank, the monkey jumped to safety, rebuked the crocodile for his treachery, and never trusted him again. YouTube YouTube +3
